    Section 3.4 Summary of Graphical Methods

    This section combines our tools for evaluating first and second derivatives to form a comprehensive, step-by-step strategy for analyzing and drawing accurate function curves.
    🔗

    Method for Sketching Graphs.

    To systematically analyze and sketch the graph of a function \(f\text{,}\) follow these steps:
    1. Determine the domain and analyze the continuity of \(f\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the \(x\)-intercepts and \(y\)-intercept.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Compute the first derivative \(f'\) and second derivative \(f''\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Find the critical points of the function.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Determine the intervals of increase and decrease, and locate local (relative) extrema.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Find the candidate inflection points.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Determine the intervals of concavity.
      🔗
      🔗
    8. Identify vertical, horizontal, or slant asymptotes, if any exist.
      🔗
      🔗
    9. Sketch the graph by plotting the key points and connecting them according to the established behaviors.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 256.

    Graph the function \(f\) given by:
    \begin{equation*} f(x)=x^3+3x^2-9x-12 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    1. Determine the domain and continuity of \(f\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find \(f'\) and \(f''\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Find the critical points.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Determine intervals of increase and decrease, and locate relative extrema.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Find inflection points and determine concavity.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Sketch the graph.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.
    1. Domain and Continuity: As a polynomial function, the domain of \(f\) is all real numbers, \((-\infty, \infty)\text{,}\) and it is continuous everywhere.
    🔗
    2. Intercepts:
    • The \(y\)-intercept is found at \(f(0) = -12\text{,}\) giving the coordinate point \((0, -12)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Setting \(f(x) = 0\) and solving \(x^3+3x^2-9x-12 = 0\) yields three real roots near \(x \approx -3.85\text{,}\) \(x \approx -1.14\text{,}\) and \(x \approx 2.00\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    3. Derivatives:
    \begin{equation*} f'(x) = 3x^2 + 6x - 9 \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} f''(x) = 6x + 6 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    4. Critical Points: Setting \(f'(x) = 0\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} 3(x^2 + 2x - 3) = 0 \implies 3(x+3)(x-1) = 0 \end{equation*}
    The interior critical numbers are \(x = -3\) and \(x = 1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    5. Relative Extrema: We analyze the sign changes of \(f'(x)\) across the domain intervals:
    🔗
    Interval \((-\infty, -3)\) \((-3, 1)\) \((1, \infty)\)
    Sign of \(f'(x)\) \(\gt 0\) \(\lt 0\) \(\gt 0\)
    Behavior of \(f\) Increasing \(\nearrow\) Decreasing \(\searrow\) Increasing \(\nearrow\)
    By the First Derivative Test, \(f\) has a local maximum at \(x = -3\) with value \(f(-3) = 15\text{,}\) and a local minimum at \(x = 1\) with value \(f(1) = -17\text{.}\)
    🔗
    6. Inflection Points and Concavity: Setting \(f''(x) = 0\) gives \(6x + 6 = 0 \implies x = -1\text{.}\)
    • On \((-\infty, -1)\text{,}\) \(f''(x) \lt 0\text{,}\) so the graph is concave down (\(\cap\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • On \((-1, \infty)\text{,}\) \(f''(x) \gt 0\text{,}\) so the graph is concave up (\(\cup\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    Since concavity changes direction, \((-1, f(-1)) = (-1, -1)\) is a point of inflection.
    🔗

    Checkpoint 261.

    Graph the function \(f\) given by:
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = xe^x \end{equation*}
    🔗
    1. Determine the domain, continuity, and asymptotic behavior of \(f\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Find the \(x\)- and \(y\)-intercepts.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. Find \(f'\) and \(f''\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Find the critical points.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Determine intervals of increase and decrease, and locate relative extrema.
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Find inflection points and determine concavity.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Sketch the graph.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Solution.

    (a) Domain, Continuity, and Asymptotes.

    The domain of \(f\) consists of all real numbers, \((-\infty, \infty)\text{,}\) and the function is continuous everywhere. As \(x \to \infty\text{,}\) \(f(x) \to \infty\text{.}\) By L’Hôpital’s Rule, the behavior as \(x \to -\infty\) is evaluated as:
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{x \to -\infty} xe^x = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{x}{e^{-x}} = \lim_{x \to -\infty} \frac{1}{-e^{-x}} = 0 \end{equation*}
    Thus, there is a horizontal asymptote at \(y = 0\) as \(x \to -\infty\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (b) Intercepts.

    • The \(y\)-intercept is found at \(f(0) = 0 \cdot e^0 = 0\text{,}\) yielding the coordinate point \((0, 0)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    • Setting \(f(x) = 0\) means solving \(xe^x = 0\text{.}\) Since \(e^x \neq 0\) for all real values, the only solution is \(x = 0\text{.}\) The single \(x\)-intercept is at \((0, 0)\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c) Derivatives.

    Using the Product Rule:
    \begin{equation*} f'(x) = e^x + xe^x = (x + 1)e^x \end{equation*}
    \begin{equation*} f''(x) = e^x + (x + 1)e^x = (x + 2)e^x \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    (d) Critical Points.

    Setting the first derivative to zero:
    \begin{equation*} (x + 1)e^x = 0 \end{equation*}
    Since \(e^x \gt 0\text{,}\) the only critical number is \(x = -1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (e) Relative Extrema.

    We analyze the sign changes of \(f'(x)\) across the domain intervals:
    🔗
    Interval \((-\infty, -1)\) \((-1, \infty)\)
    Sign of \(f'(x)\) \(\lt 0\) \(\gt 0\)
    Behavior of \(f\) Decreasing \(\searrow\) Increasing \(\nearrow\)
    By the First Derivative Test, \(f\) has a local minimum at \(x = -1\) with value \(f(-1) = -1e^{-1} = -\dfrac{1}{e} \approx -0.368\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    (f) Inflection Points and Concavity.

    Setting \(f''(x) = 0\) yields \((x + 2)e^x = 0 \implies x = -2\text{.}\)
    • On \((-\infty, -2)\text{,}\) \(f''(x) \lt 0\text{,}\) so the graph is concave down (\(\cap\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    • On \((-2, \infty)\text{,}\) \(f''(x) \gt 0\text{,}\) so the graph is concave up (\(\cup\)).
      🔗
      🔗
    Since the concavity changes direction, the inflection point is located at \((-2, f(-2)) = \left(-2, -2e^{-2}\right) = \left(-2, -\dfrac{2}{e^2}\right) \approx (-2, -0.271)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    Solution.

    (a) Local Extrema and Inflection Points.

    To find the critical numbers, we set the first derivative equal to zero:
    \begin{equation*} 16 - x^2 = 0 \implies (4 - x)(4 + x) = 0 \end{equation*}

    Behavior of \(f\) Decreasing \(\searrow\) Increasing \(\nearrow\) Decreasing \(\searrow\)
    By the First Derivative Test, \(f\) has a local minimum at \(x = -4\) and a local maximum at \(x = 4\).
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} y'' = -2x \end{equation*}
    Setting \(y'' = 0\) yields \(x = 0\text{.}\) Since \(y'' \gt 0\) for \(x \lt 0\) (concave up) and \(y'' \lt 0\) for \(x \gt 0\) (concave down), the graph has an inflection point at \(x = 0\).
