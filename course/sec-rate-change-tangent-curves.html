    Section Rate of Change and Tangent Curves

    The rate of change of a function at a point is the slope of the tangent line to the graph of the function at that point. It can be calculated using limits.
    🔗
    If I drive 200 miles in 4 hours, my average velocity is \(50\) miles per hour. Let \(s(t)\) represent the reading on my odometer at time \(t\text{.}\) Suppose I want to find my average velocity during some time interval that begins when \(t=a\) and ends \(h\) hours later. Velocity can be calculated as follows:
    \begin{equation*} v_{\text{ave}} = \frac{\text{change in distance}}{\text{change in time}} = \frac{s(a+h)-s(a)}{h} \end{equation*}
    The subscript “ave” tells us this is an average velocity. My velocity does not have to be constant during this time (Even cruise control is not perfect).
    🔗
    Our common notion of velocity involves a change in distance and a change in time. Yet, a speedometer tells us the velocity of a car at any instant. We can do this mathematically using the idea of a limit.
    🔗

    Average Rate of Change.

    The average rate of change of \(y\) with respect to \(x\), as \(x\) changes from \(x_1\) to \(x_2\text{,}\) is the ratio of the change in output to the change in input:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}, \quad \text{where } x_2 \neq x_1 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    The slope of the line from \(P(x_1,y_1)\) to \(Q(x_2,y_2)\) is \(\displaystyle \frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}\) and the line \(\overleftrightarrow{PQ}\) is called a secant line.
    🔗

    Definition 67. Tangent Line.

    The Tangent line to the curve \(y=f(x)\) at the point \(P(a,f(a))\) is the line through \(P\) with slope
    \begin{equation*} m = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a} \end{equation*}
    provided that this limit exists.
    🔗
    🔗
    We sometimes refer to the slope of the tangent line to the curve at a point as the slope of the curve at the point.
    🔗

    Example 68.

    Find the equation of the tangent line to the parabola \(y=x^2\) at the point \(P(-2,4)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution. Method using \(x \to a\)
    We can also find the slope \(m\) at \(a = -2\) using the alternative limit definition:
    \begin{align*} m \amp = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x) - f(a)}{x - a}\\ \amp = \lim_{x \to -2} \frac{x^2 - (-2)^2}{x - (-2)}\\ \amp = \lim_{x \to -2} \frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Factoring the numerator as a difference of squares:
    \begin{align*} \amp = \lim_{x \to -2} \frac{(x + 2)(x - 2)}{x + 2}\\ \amp = \lim_{x \to -2} (x - 2)\\ \amp = -2 - 2 = -4 \end{align*}
    🔗
    This gives the same slope, \(m = -4\text{,}\) which we then use in the point-slope formula to find \(y = -4x - 4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    The average rate of change is called a difference quotient, given by:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} = \frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1} = \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{(x+h)-x} = \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    We now use a different notation for average rates of change by eliminating the subscripts. Let \(x\) be \(x_1\) and \(x+h\) be \(x_2\text{.}\) To get from \(x_1\) to \(x_2\text{,}\) we calculate the change in input as \(x_2 - x_1 = (x+h) - x = h\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Definition 70. Difference Quotient and Tangent Slope.

    The average rate of change of \(f\) with respect to \(x\) is also called the difference quotient. It is given by:
    \begin{equation*} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}, \quad \text{where } h \neq 0 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    The difference quotient is equal to the slope of the line from a point \(P(x,f(x))\) to a point \(Q(x+h,f(x+h))\text{.}\) The slope of the tangent line at \(x=a\) is then:
    \begin{equation*} m = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 71. Rate of Change of a Quadratic Function.

    Consider the function
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = x^2\text{.} \end{equation*}
    We want to find the rate of change at the point
    \begin{equation*} x=-2\text{.} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Using the definition, we have:
    🔗
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{(-2+h)^2 - (-2)^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{4 - 4h + h^2 - 4}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-4h + h^2}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} (-4 + h) = -4 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Therefore, the rate of change of the function at
    \begin{equation*} x=-2 \end{equation*}
    is -4.
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 72.

    Find the slope of the curve \(y = 1/x^2\) at \(x = 1/2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, we find the \(y\)-coordinate of the point:
    \begin{equation*} y = f(1/2) = \frac{1}{(1/2)^2} = 4 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Then, we find a formula for the \(y\)-coordinate at a point near \(x = 1/2\text{.}\) By squaring the binomial and simplifying:
    \begin{equation*} f(1/2+h) = \frac{1}{(1/2+h)^2} = \frac{1}{1/4 + h + h^2} = \frac{4}{1 + 4h + 4h^2} \end{equation*}
    🔗
    Now, we evaluate the limit of the difference quotient:
    \begin{align*} f'(1/2) \amp = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{\frac{4}{1 + 4h + 4h^2} - 4}{h}\\ \amp = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{\frac{4}{1 + 4h + 4h^2} - \frac{4(1 + 4h + 4h^2)}{1 + 4h + 4h^2}}{h}\\ \amp = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{\frac{4 - 4 - 16h - 16h^2}{1 + 4h + 4h^2}}{h}\\ \amp = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{-16h - 16h^2}{h(1 + 4h + 4h^2)}\\ \amp = \lim_{h\to 0} \frac{-16 - 16h}{1 + 4h + 4h^2}\\ \amp = \frac{-16 - 0}{1 + 0 + 0} = -16 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Our calculation shows that the slope of the line tangent to \(y = 1/x^2\) at \(x = 1/2\) is \(-16\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Example 73.

    For \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) find a simplified form of the difference quotient.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Recall that the difference quotient is given by \(\displaystyle \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\text{.}\) Substituting \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) we have:
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \amp = \frac{(x+h)^2 - x^2}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{(x^2 + 2xh + h^2) - x^2}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{2xh + h^2}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{h(2x + h)}{h}\\ \amp = 2x + h, \quad \text{for } h \neq 0 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    By taking the limit of the result from Example 73 as \(h \to 0\text{,}\) we find the derivative (the instantaneous rate of change) of the function.
    🔗

    The Derivative of \(x^2\).

    The derivative of \(f(x) = x^2\text{,}\) denoted as \(f'(x)\text{,}\) is calculated as:
    \begin{align*} f'(x) \amp = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\\ \amp = \lim_{h \to 0} (2x + h)\\ \amp = 2x \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    This result, \(f'(x) = 2x\text{,}\) provides a formula to find the slope of the tangent line to the parabola at any point \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Example 74.

    For \(f(x)=x^3\text{,}\) find a simplified form of the difference quotient.
    🔗
    Solution.
    We substitute \(f(x)=x^3\) into the difference quotient formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \amp = \frac{(x+h)^3 - x^3}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{(x^3 + 3x^2h + 3xh^2 + h^3) - x^3}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{3x^2h + 3xh^2 + h^3}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{h(3x^2 + 3xh + h^2)}{h}\\ \amp = 3x^2 + 3xh + h^2, \quad \text{for } h \neq 0 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    By taking the limit of the result from Example 74 as \(h \to 0\text{,}\) we find the derivative (the instantaneous rate of change) of the function.
    🔗
    This result, \(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} (3x^2 + 3xh + h^2)=3x^2\text{,}\) provides a formula to find the slope of the tangent line to the cubic curve at any point \(x\text{.}\)
    🔗

    Checkpoint 75.

    (a)

    For \(f(x)=3/x\text{,}\) find a simplified form of the difference quotient.
    🔗
    Solution.
    We substitute \(f(x) = \frac{3}{x}\) into the difference quotient formula:
    \begin{align*} \frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h} \amp = \frac{\frac{3}{x+h} - \frac{3}{x}}{h}\\ \amp = \frac{1}{h} \left( \frac{3x - 3(x+h)}{x(x+h)} \right)\\ \amp = \frac{1}{h} \left( \frac{3x - 3x - 3h}{x(x+h)} \right)\\ \amp = \frac{-3h}{h \cdot x(x+h)}\\ \amp = \frac{-3}{x(x+h)}, \quad \text{for } h \neq 0 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Find the equation of the tangent line to the hyperbola \(y=3/x\) at the point \(P(-1,-3)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, we find the slope \(m\) at \(x = -1\) by taking the limit of the difference quotient from the previous part:
    \begin{align*} m \amp = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-3}{x(x+h)} \bigg|_{x=-1}\\ \amp = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-3}{-1(-1+h)}\\ \amp = \frac{-3}{(-1)(-1)} = -3 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Now, using the point-slope form with \(P(-1, -3)\) and \(m = -3\text{:}\)
    \begin{align*} y - (-3) \amp = -3(x - (-1))\\ y + 3 \amp = -3x - 3\\ y \amp = -3x - 6 \end{align*}
    🔗
    The equation of the tangent line is \(y = -3x - 6\text{.}\)
