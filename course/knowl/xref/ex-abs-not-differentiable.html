Example 87.

If \(f(x)=|x|\text{,}\) show that \(f\) is not differentiable at \(0\text{.}\)
🔗
Solution.
To show that \(f(x) = |x|\) is not differentiable at \(x = 0\text{,}\) we must check if the limit of the difference quotient exists at that point:
\begin{equation*} f'(0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(0+h) - f(0)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{|h| - |0|}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{|h|}{h} \end{equation*}
🔗
We examine the one-sided limits:
🔗
  • From the right (\(h \to 0^+\)), \(|h| = h\text{,}\) so:
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{h}{h} = 1 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
  • From the left (\(h \to 0^-\)), \(|h| = -h\text{,}\) so:
    \begin{equation*} \lim_{h \to 0^-} \frac{-h}{h} = -1 \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
Since the left-hand and right-hand limits are not equal (\(1 \neq -1\)), the two-sided limit does not exist. Therefore, \(f(x) = |x|\) is not differentiable at \(x = 0\text{.}\) This corresponds to the sharp corner (or cusp) seen at the origin on its graph.
